## Resources
The overall state of a PC is manifested through three values:

- **Hit** Points (*HP*) represent how many blows, grazes and near misses a PC can sustain before suffering serious injury or mortal wounds. Each time a PC would suffer damage, normally due to an attack, they lose HP (*based on the ferocity of the assault*). 

- **Action** Points (*AP*) represent the energy of a PC, and the frequency with which they can exert themselves or attempt flourishes during their actions. A PC must spend AP (*based on the action taken*) to perform manoeuvres and utilise weapon masteries.
-
- **Magic** Points (*MP*) represent a PC's connection to the mystical, and their ability to draw from it to shape reality. Each time a PC would cast a spell, or activate another form of magical power, they must spend MP to do so (*based on the potency of the spell*).
#### Depleted Conditions
Whilst the value of a resource is equal to or below half of it's maximum total, a special [[108 Combat#Conditions|condition]] is applied to the PC (*i.e. Bloodied, Fatigued, Drained*).

These conditions represent visible indicators that PCs can discern and react to. Certain powers, events or enemy behaviors can trigger when these conditions are gained (*or lost*).
#### Temporary Vitality
In some situations, a PC may recover more vitality than is required. As a PC's resources cannot exceed their maximum value, any additional healing is lost.

However, certain medicinal and magical sources may provide ways for a PC to go beyond their maximum vitality thresholds, by providing temporary resources. 

A temporary resource (*i.e. temporary HP*) is an additional value alongside the core thresholds, and once depleted, it cannot be recovered or refreshed.

Generating new temporary resources immediately replaces any existing value of the same type (*regardless of whether they have been spent or not*). 

All temporary resources are lost when commencing a [[104 Vitality#Rest & Recovery|rest]].

## Rest & Recovery
A PC can naturally heal and recover lost vitality by resting in a safe, comfortable space.
The better the conditions, the faster their vigor will return.

To perform a rest check, the DM & PCs agree on the safety of their chosen resting spot.

|  Location  | Recovery TN |             Examples              |
| :--------: | :---------: | :-------------------------------: |
|    Safe    |      5      |  Own Home, Faction Headquarters   |
|   Secure   |     10      |    Local Tavern, Queen's Keep     |
| Vulnerable |     15      | Woodland Clearing, City Alleyway  |
| Dangerous  |     20      |      Dark Cave, Steep Cliff       |
|  Hostile   |     25      | Bandit's Camp, Necromancer's Lair |
|   Deadly   |     30      |   Battlefield, Erupting Volcano   |

The PCs will determine how long they intend on relaxing for. The DM will alter the TN accordingly, based on the desired [[104 Vitality#Resting Duration|rest duration]].

In addition to basic relaxation, the PCs may utilise certain comforts and luxuries to further improve their recovery:

- Water - Having fresh, clean water to drink
- Food - Having something to eat (*i.e. rations*)
- Warmth - Having a safe source of heat (*i.e. a campfire*)
- Shelter -  Having protection from the elements (*i.e. a tent*)
- Entertainment - Having some way to pass the time (*i.e. a lute, playing cards, a book*)

For each of the comforts that apply to all the PCs in the group (*including those [[104 Vitality#On Watch|on watch]]*), they upgrade the resting (*proficiency*) die by one rank.

Once the proficiency die has been determined, one of the PCs is nominated to make the check on behalf of the group. They roll a (*d20*) check, alongside the resting die, and inform the DM of their final (*total*) result.

The DM subtracts the original TN from the PC's final result, and awards Vitality Points (*VP*) to equal to the difference (*negative values are ignored*). Each PC may spend that amount of VP to restore hit points (*HP*), action points (*AP*) or magic points (*MP*) in any combination they desire (*at a one-for-one exchange rate*).

All unused vitality points are lost once the rest is concluded.

#### Duration
When resting, PCs may try and regroup swiftly, or take their time and relax as much as possible. The DM may determine certain timescales impossible due to environmental or narrative reasons.

Based on the PC's choice, the chances to regain vitality points may differ. A safe haven won't offer much recovery if the group leaves too soon. Likewise, spending a long time in an uncomfortable place may allow for that little bit extra rejuvenation the group requires.

| **Quick** | **Short** | **Standard** | **Long** |  **Extended**   |
| :-------: | :-------: | :----------: | :------: | :-------------: |
|    +10    |    +5     |      0       |    -5    |       -10       |
| *45mins*  | *90mins*  |    *3hrs*    |  *6hrs*  |     *12hrs*     |

> [!tip] The timings suggested above are guidelines, correlating in scale with downtime actions (*i.e. respite*).

#### Interrupted Rest
If the PCs fail the check (*i.e. their result is below the TN*), then they will suffer an interrupted rest. This can take many forms and vary in threat, from woodland creatures stealing food to full scale raiding parties on the hunt for blood.

The DM will use the location and the duration of the PC's rest to determine what an encounter might be. Shorter rests in safer locations often present much smaller threats than longer rests in more dangerous surroundings.

> [!warning] An extended rest in a dangerous environment might seem like the best option to recover some much needed vitality, but the chances of encountering a deadly foe increases exponentially the longer the PCs stay in that perilous location.

Regardless, if a rest is interrupted, the PCs do not gain any vitality points (VP). They also may be unable to rest again, depending on how much time has elapsed or other circumstances.
#### On Watch
A PC may volunteer to forgo full resting and instead, provide added protection for their party, by taking on the roll of lookout or sentry. A PC 'on watch' is only awarded half of the VP they are entitled too after a successful rest, however, if the rest is interrupted, they will automatically gain the initiative, allowing them to raise the alarm and take the first action if they wish.

## Afflictions
Beyond the recovery of vitality, there are other types of ailments that may affect a PC. These require attention as they manifest negative consequences until they are dealt with:

- **Diseases** reduce resilience, and a PC's abilities. They are fought over time, by the body's natural constitution, which in turn, can be helped by general medicines.
- **Poisons** affect the defences of a PC, making them more vulnerable. To remove poison, a PC needs to find a relevant antitoxin or a potion that explicitly targets poisons.
- **Curses** can do a variety of things, often triggered by a particular action or event. To remove a curse, a PC must vanquish it's source, or locate a magical or religious healer.

## Exhaustion
A PC may acquire exhaustion in many ways. Most notably whilst travelling, after performing arduous tasks, or when they are near death during combat.

  > [!warning|noicon] If a PC reaches 0 HP and receives any further damage, they suffer exhaustion instead (*equal to the damage dealt*). All other effects are applied as normal.

For each rank of exhaustion they have, a PC reduces the total result of every check they make by one. There is no limit to how much exhaustion a PC can receive, although once they surpass a high threshold (*i.e. -20*), a PC is virtually useless to their party, so effort should be made to keep this value to a minimum.

A PC cannot heal HP (*by any means*) whilst they have exhaustion. Instead, ranks of exhaustion are removed first, until there are none remaining, and then HP may be restored.
## Death
> [!danger|noicon] If a PC takes a critical hit whilst on 0 HP, they die.

If this happens, play is immediately halted, and the dying PC may perform one final action before they are removed from the game.

If they choose to do something requiring a check, they may add an additional d12 to that roll (*along with their regular proficiency die*) to symbolise their last gasp of heroic effort.

> [!tip] The DM may allow prominent NPCs to enact a similar final stand.

#### Incapacitated
If an NPC reaches 0 HP, a PC may attempt to 


(*i.e. tie them up, knock them unconscious, remove their arcane focus* ), instead of killing them. If chosen to do so, the NPC gains a relevant condition until the current encounter is resolved, after which, the DM and PC will determine the fate of the NPC through narrative play.

> [!warning|noicon] Based on an NPC's objectives, they may prefer to incapacitate a PC rather than kill them. The DM will make the PCs aware of this preference at the beginning of the encounter.
