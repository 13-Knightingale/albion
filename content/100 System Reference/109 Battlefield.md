## Zones
The battlefield is a collection of abstract areas, known as zones.

> [!tip|noicon] As there is no set size for combat zones, the DM should ensure all zones in a combat, and the relative distances between them, are roughly equal. A large area may be made up of several contiguous zones to account for this. 

Each zone may contain multiple PCs (*& NPCs*), and may have interactable objects that the DM may describe as the battle progresses. Each zone will be clearly distinct, usually having a landmark (*i.e. Fireplace, Altar,* *Large Oak Tree*) to help the PCs further identify boundaries and ranges.

Certain zones are connected, which represents the capability for a PC to move between them with a move action.

> [!warning] Not all zones connect, making traversing the battlefield of keen tactical importance.

The [[108 Combat#Range Bands|range bands]] of weapons, spells and certain powers are calculated based on the relative positioning of the PC (and their target) in zones.

## Terrain
Not all zones are easy to traverse, stand or balance upon. Different terrain types affect the ability to move through them.

- **Difficult Terrain**: Melee attacks within a zone require the PC to spend a move action, alongside their main action. Moving through (out) of the zone requires two move actions.
  (*i.e. Bog, River, Crowd*)

- **Hazardous Terrain**: At the end of each round, the DM will roll a hazard (==*trap*==) check, targeting each PC (*& NPC*) in the zone. Moving into the zone triggers an additional check. 
  (*i.e. Lava, Gas, Nest*)

> [!warning] Terrain types can stack, allowing a zone to be both difficult and hazardous!

## Elevation
Battlefields aren't perfectly flat. Each zone will have an elevation level, indicating it's height relative to the zones around it.

> [!tip] Usually, the most common elevation level on the battlefield is denoted as level zero. However, the DM may choose the assign level zero to the lowest possible point on the battlefield instead, in order to avoid 'negative' elevation levels.

When targeting zones of a lower elevation, the PC gains advantage to all actions, and increases the distance short range weapons can reach by an additional zone.

> [!warning] The DM may deem short range attacks impossible, if the elevation is great enough.

Holding higher ground may also reduce the TN of particular checks (*i.e. detecting movement*).
#### Falling
If a PC moves to a zone that is more than one elevation level below their current zone, they will fall. A PC loses 1HP per difference in elevation beyond that first level (*i.e. falling from elevation level 4 to level 1 loses the PC 2HP*). A PC may deliberately jump as a main action (*making an ability check*) to try and reduce or negate this damage.

> [!tip] Once again, there are no defined parameters for elevation levels. However, to maintain balance, it is recommended each level be equal to a medium sized character.

## Cover
 Rarely will a battlefield be empty. There are many things (*i.e. furniture, shrubbery, walls*) that can make it difficult for attacks to hit their target.
 
 A PC may spend their movement action to take cover. This represents the PC shifting their position, to utilise the cover available within their current zone (*if any*). The benefits a PC receives depend on the size and quality of cover used:

- **Partial Cover** (*also known as Soft Cover*): Provides the PC with protection against ranged attacks. Any attacks made from outside of the PC's zone are made with disadvantage.

- **Full Cover** (*also known as Hard Cover*): Provides the PC with protection against all incoming attacks. Any attack made against the PC is done so with disadvantage.

> [!warning|noicon] The PC taking cover is also subject to these restrictions (*i.e. if a PC takes partial cover, any ranged attacks attempted whilst in cover are made with disadvantage*).

#### Breaking Cover
It is possible for a PC to utilise the same cover as an enemy, or force them back out into the open. When making an attack, a PC may spend a movement action (*alongside their main action*) to temporally nullify the benefits their target receives from cover.

The attack is made as normal, but if the PC succeeds, they may choose (*alongside doing damage*) to push an enemy out of cover entirely. Alternatively, they may remain in their current position, and gain the cover for themselves also.

> [!tip] A PC may directly target the source of cover, potentially permanently destroying it.

## Concealment
Environments and combatants may create areas of restricted vision on the battlefield (*i.e. dense forest, wall of smoke, shifting crowd).

A PC does not need to take any action to utilise concealment, they are automatically affected, providing they remain in the appropriate zone. The benefits a PC receives depend on the type of concealment:

- **Partially Concealed** (*also known as Obscured*): All attacks (*ranged and melee*) targeting a PC in this zone will trigger ==friendly fire== on a miss, regardless of weapon (*or spell*) qualities.

- **Fully Concealed** (*also known as Hidden*): NA PC in this zone cannot be directly targeted by any action (*positive or negative*). The PC cannot target anyone else until moving out of the zone (*or the concealment is removed*). (==LINE OF SIGHT==)

> [!warning] Both forms of concealment may affect a PC, even if they are not directly aiming into that zone, providing the concealed zone is between the PC and their desired target.

Concealment may also reduce the TN of particular checks (*i.e. identifying creatures*).

#### Line Of Sight
When taking actions, a PC cannot target anyone they cannot see. The PC may be treated as standing anywhere within their current zone, and their target anywhere within theirs, when determining line of sight. Ultimately, it is an abstract measurement that may require discussion or a ruling by the DM to adjudicate. 

> [!warning] Some NPCs will supplement sight with other senses, allowing them to bypass common forms of concealment, or attack from unexpected angles.

Some actions do not require line of sight (*these will be explicitly noted*).

> [!tip|noicon] If a PC chooses to attack a zone they cannot see (*or sense*), this attack should be made with disadvantage, and the DM may randomise the target within the chosen zone.
