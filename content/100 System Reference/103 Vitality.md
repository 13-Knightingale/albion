## Resources
The overall state of a PC is manifested through three values:

- **Hit** Points (*HP*) represent how many blows, grazes and near misses a PC can sustain before suffering serious injury or mortal wounds. Each time a PC would suffer [[108 Combat#Damage Types|damage]], usually due to an [[108 Combat#Making An Attack|attack]], they lose HP (*based on the ferocity of the assault*). 
<br />
- **Action** Points (*AP*) represent the energy of a PC, and the frequency with which they can exert themselves or attempt flourishes during their encounters. A PC must spend AP (*based on the intricacy of the manoeuvre*) to perform [[108 Combat#Stunts|stunts]] and utilise weapon [[106 Inventory#Equipping Gear|qualities]].
<br />
- **Magic** Points (*MP*) represent a PC's connection to the mystical, and their ability to draw from it to shape reality. Each time a PC would cast a [[110 Spellcasting#Casting A Spell|spell]], or activate another form of magical [[201 Character Creation#Powers|power]], they must spend MP to do so (*based on the potency of the spell*).

<br />

#### Depleted Conditions
Whilst the value of a resource is equal to or below half of it's maximum total, a special [[108 Combat#Conditions|condition]] is applied to the PC (*i.e. Bloodied, Fatigued, Drained*).

> [!success|noicon] These conditions represent visible indicators that can be discerned and reacted to. As such, PCs must indicate when they are affected by these conditions, likewise, the DM will reveal when an NPC crosses these thresholds.

Certain powers, events or enemy behaviors can trigger when these conditions are gained (*or lost*).

<br />

#### Temporary Vitality
In some situations, a PC may recover more vitality than is required. As a PC's resources cannot exceed their maximum value, any additional healing is lost.

However, certain [[106 Inventory#Equipping Gear|medicinal]] and [[110 Spellcasting#Casting A Spell|magical]] sources may provide ways for a PC to go beyond their maximum vitality thresholds, by providing temporary resources. 

A temporary resource (*i.e. temporary HP*) is an additional value alongside the core thresholds, and once depleted, it cannot be recovered or refreshed.

Generating new temporary resources immediately replaces any existing value of the same type (*regardless of whether they have been spent or not*). 

All temporary resources are lost when commencing a [[103 Vitality#Rest & Recovery|rest]].

<br />

## Rest & Recovery
A PC can naturally heal and recover lost vitality by resting in a safe, comfortable space.
The better the conditions, the faster their vigor will return.

To perform a rest check, the DM will determine the safety of the chosen resting spot.

<br />

|  Location  | Recovery TN |             Examples              |
| :--------: | :---------: | :-------------------------------: |
|    Safe    |      5      |  Own Home, Faction Headquarters   |
|   Secure   |     10      |    Local Tavern, Queen's Keep     |
| Vulnerable |     15      | Woodland Clearing, City Alleyway  |
| Dangerous  |     20      |      Dark Cave, Steep Cliff       |
|  Hostile   |     25      | Bandit's Camp, Necromancer's Lair |
|   Deadly   |     30      |   Battlefield, Erupting Volcano   |

<br />

The PCs will then decide how long they intend on relaxing for. The DM will alter the TN accordingly, based on the desired [[103 Vitality#Resting Duration|rest duration]].

In addition to basic relaxation, the PCs may utilise certain comforts and luxuries to further improve their recovery:

- **Water**: Having fresh, clean water to drink
- **Food**: Having something to eat (*i.e. rations*)
- **Warmth**: Having a safe source of heat (*i.e. a campfire*)
- **Shelter**: Having protection from the weather (*i.e. a tent*)
- **Entertainment**: Having some way to pass the time (*i.e. a lute, playing cards, a book*)

For each of the comforts that apply to the whole the party (*including those [[103 Vitality#On Watch|on watch]]*), the resting (*[[101 D20 Mechanics#Proficiency Dice|proficiency]]*) die is upgraded by one rank.

Once the proficiency die has been determined, one of the PCs is nominated to make the check on behalf of the party. They roll a d20, alongside the resting die, and inform the DM of their final (*total*) result.

The DM subtracts the TN from the PC's final result and, if the result is positive, awards Vitality Points (*VP*) to each PC, equal to the difference. Each PC may spend their VP to restore hit points (*HP*), action points (*AP*) or magic points (*MP*) in any combination (*at a one-for-one exchange rate*).

All unused vitality points are lost once the rest is concluded.

<br />

#### Duration
A safe haven won't offer much recovery if the party leaves too soon. On the contrary, spending a long time in an uncomfortable place might allow for that much needed rejuvenation.

When resting, the PCs must decide whether to regroup swiftly, or take their time and relax as much as possible (*or something in-between*). The DM may determine certain timescales impossible due to environmental or narrative reasons.

<br />

| **Quick** | **Short** | **Standard** | **Long** |  **Extended**   |
| :-------: | :-------: | :----------: | :------: | :-------------: |
|    +10    |    +5     |      0       |    -5    |       -10       |
| *45mins*  | *90mins*  |    *3hrs*    |  *6hrs*  |     *12hrs*     |

<br />
<br />

#### Interrupted Rest
If the PCs fail the check (*their result is below the TN*), then they will suffer an interrupted rest. 

This can take many forms and vary in threat, from woodland creatures stealing food to full scale raiding parties on the hunt for blood. The DM will use the location and duration of the rest to determine what an encounter might be. Shorter rests in safer locations often present much smaller threats than longer rests in more dangerous surroundings.

> [!warning] An extended rest in a dangerous environment might seem like the best option to recover maximum vitality, but the chances of encountering a deadly foe increases exponentially the longer the PCs stay in that perilous location.

Regardless, if a rest is interrupted, the PCs do not gain any vitality points (VP). The DM may also deem further rest impossible, based on a variety of factors (*i.e. elapsed time*).

<br />

#### On Watch
A PC may volunteer to forgo full resting and instead, provide added protection for their party, taking on the roll of lookout or sentry. If the party successfully rests, a PC 'on watch' is only awarded half of the VP they are entitled to. If the rest is interrupted, they will automatically gain the initiative, allowing them to raise the alarm and take the first action.

<br />

## Afflictions
Beyond the recovery of vitality, there are other types of ailments that may affect a PC. These require attention as they manifest negative consequences until they are dealt with:

- **Diseases** reduce a PC's [[102 Characteristics#Abilities|abilities]], hindering their skills. They are fought over time, by the body's natural constitution, which in turn, can be helped by general [[106 Inventory#Equipping Gear|medicines]].
<br />
- **Poisons** affect the [[102 Characteristics#Defences|defences]] of a PC, making them more vulnerable. To remove poison, a PC needs to acquire the corresponding antitoxin or a broader healing [[106 Inventory#Equipping Gear|potion]].
<br />
- **Curses** can do a variety of things, often triggered by a particular action or event. To remove a curse, a PC must vanquish it's source, or locate a relevant expert (*i.e. [[110 Spellcasting#Casting A Spell|magical]], [[201 Character Creation#Deity|religious]] or [[201 Character Creation#Primal|spiritual]]*) for further guidance.

<br />

## Exhaustion
A PC may acquire exhaustion in many ways, most notably by travelling long distances, performing arduous tasks, or when facing fearsome foes in combat.

  > [!warning|noicon] When a PC reaches 0 HP and would suffer further damage, they gain ranks of exhaustion instead (*equal to the damage dealt*). All other effects are applied as normal.

A PC reduces the total result of every check they make by one for each rank of exhaustion they currently have. There is no limit to how much exhaustion a PC can withstand, although once they surpass a high threshold (*i.e. -20*), a PC is virtually useless to their party, so effort should be made to keep this value to a minimum.

A PC cannot heal HP (*by any means*) whilst they have exhaustion. Instead, ranks of exhaustion are removed first, until there are none remaining, and then HP may be restored.

<br />

## Death

> [!danger|noicon] If a PC is struck by a critical hit whilst on 0 HP, they die.

If this happens, play is immediately halted, and the dying PC may perform one final action before they are removed from the game.

If they choose to do something requiring a check, they may add an additional d12 to that roll (*along with their regular proficiency die*) to symbolise their last gasp of heroic effort.

> [!tip] The DM may allow prominent NPCs to enact a similar final stand.

When an NPC reaches 0 HP, they die (*no [[101 D20 Mechanics#Critical Hit|critical hit]] required*).
