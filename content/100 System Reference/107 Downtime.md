## Downtime Activities
Whenever the PCs have no direct goals to strive for, or the plot requires a period of waiting, the DM may call for downtime.

Downtime is a collection of small or simple encounters, rarely requiring more than a single check to accomplish. Each PC can take part in different types of downtime activities, creating a montage of events that represent the casual passing of time.

When a DM determines downtime is appropriate, they will award each PC with a number of downtime actions (*DA*), based on how much time the downtime is meant to represent.

> [!tip|noicon] Downtime actions are deliberately abstract, and can be scaled by the DM as desired. However, as a suggested baseline, a PC may spend up to four downtime actions per day.

The PCs will then decide what to spend their downtime actions on. They discuss their wishes with the DM, who decides whether a check is required, what the outcome of a successful check will be, and most importantly, how many downtime actions it would cost to attempt it.

> [!warning|noicon] Not every activity will be available each time the PCs are granted downtime (*i.e. some may be location dependent or based on the time of day*). The DM will determine which actions are available in any given downtime.


## Crafting

Crafting is an abstract term used to describe any action a PC takes to build, construct or create something that didn't (or wouldn't) exist prior to the PC's involvement. It most often refers to combining common materials into a useful tool or weapon (*i.e. making a spear whilst in a forest*), but can be applied to larger structures (*i.e. repairing a cabin*), mixing ingredients (*i.e. brewing a potion*) or even event planning (*i.e. organising a party*).

Regardless of goal, a DM can call for a crafting check to determine the level of success (*and the quality of anything created*). Before a PC can make a crafting check, they must overcome any restrictions that may impede (*stop*) the check from being made (*i.e. trying to forge a sword without a source of heat*).

Once a PC has gathered the necessary [[107 Downtime#Supplies|supplies]] and [[107 Downtime#Key Ingredients|key ingredient]] for their desired recipe, they roll their (*d20*) check, alongside the crafting die. The rank (*size*) of the crafting die is determined based on several factors:

- **Preparation**: If the crafting check is performed as a downtime action (*1DA*), increase the crafting die rank by one.
- **Guidance**: If the crafting is done under the supervision of an expert, or by following explicit instructions, increase the crafting die rank by one.
- **Equipment**: If the crafting is performed in a dedicated venue, or the PC has access to purpose made tools, increase the crafting die rank by one.
- **Safety**: If the crafting does not pose a risk to the PC (*i.e. is unlikely to harm them in the event of a mistake*), increase the crafting die rank by one.
- **Assistance**: If other PCs (*or NPCs*) are available to help, increase the crafting die rank by one.

Supplies and key ingredients are consumed each time a PC attempts a crafting check.

A crafting roll can never fail, but may produce a subpar result. Based on the total value rolled, the DM will determine the quality (*and sometimes quantity*) of the PC's endeavors.

|                 | **TN** |                                          |
| :-------------: | :----: | :--------------------------------------- |
|    **Poor**     |   5    | *Less than intended, likely to break*    |
|   **Average**   |   10   | *Usable and presentable, unremarkable*   |
|    **Good**     |   15   | *Solid design, beyond standard flavours* |
| **Impressive**  |   20   | *Unique or ornate features, potent*      |
| **Exceptional** |   25   | *Worthy of notice, highly sought after*  |
|  **Flawless**   |   30   | *No negative qualities, masterful craft* |
Rolling a critical [[101 D20 Mechanics#Critical Rolls|boon]] whilst crafting allows a PC to retain key ingredients for future crafts.
If a PC rolls a critical [[101 D20 Mechanics#Critical Rolls|bane]] whilst crafting, they suffer a loss of tools, venue or expertise.

> [!tip] Some crafting projects will take longer than others. The DM may determine how much time is required for a PC to attempt such a craft. Otherwise, it is assumed all crafting is performed relatively instantaneously.

#### Supplies
Supplies are a shorthand way of describing the collection of mundane ingredients that are required for crafting (*i.e. bottles for brewing, eggs for baking, candles for rituals*). 

Essentially, supplies are the levy (*cost*) associated with each crafting attempt, to provide consequence for poor results, and to discourage infinite crafting loops.

> [!tip] The DM may make acquiring supplies part of the adventure, have them sold as a package at local stores, or forgo them altogether and simply 'charge' a PC in coins whenever they make a crafting check.

If the supplies used when crafted are deemed to be of a particularly high quality (*i.e. supplies made of unique or exotic material*), the PC may roll their crafting check with advantage. However, if the supplies are of low quality (*i.e. supplies found whilst scavenging*), the PC must roll their crafting check with disadvantage.

#### Key Ingredients
The most important part of any crafting check is the key ingredient. Supplies are generic and can be utilised in many different ways, but each recipe's key ingredient is distinct. Changing the key ingredient will alter the properties of the final product (*i.e. the flavour of a cake, the durability of a shield, the destination of a portal*).

The DM will determine what the key ingredient may be for a given check. It may be simple (*i.e. chalk, leather, gemstones*), but when performing significant crafting (*i.e. forging magical weaponry*), such key ingredients should be difficult to locate, potentially requiring the PC to harvest parts from mythical creatures (*i.e. the horn of a unicorn*).

> [!success] By experimenting with various key ingredients, a PC may discover useful recipes.
